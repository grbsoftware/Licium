# Gallery

Twenty-five rolls from the liked set. None of these were designed — each one
was grown from a seed through a typed grammar, screened on the CPU, rendered
and verdicted by an automated pass, and then graded by hand.

**The filename is the seed.** `LXS4-01D397C1` is not a label attached to a
preset; it is the instruction that rebuilds it. Feed the ID back to the
generator under the generation version that made it and you get the same
preset, byte for byte. That contract held across ten versions.

## A caveat worth stating up front

These are stills of something that moves, and they undersell it — which is
not a presentation problem, it is one of the project's own findings. Nine
percent of the corpus was classified **latebloom**: dead on a quick look,
alive only after a forty-second settle. If a third of later rolls need that
long to become anything, a browsing pass never sees them do it, and neither
does a still.

Twenty-two of the captures below are quick-look frames. Three (`LX1-00004435`,
`LX7-020D4F2F`, `LXS4-01B7A8B4`) had the full settle.

## LX4 — the open scaffold

The fixed analytic medium was retired. A seed now grows *two* expressions, and
the buffer's velocity is the curl of the second — so the search reaches
arbitrary flows, and audio inside the flow potential bends the motion instead
of only brightening pixels.

## LXS1, LXS4 — the swarm lineages

Generation-side ant-colony search: a pheromone table derived from existing
verdicts biases the grammar's kind draws. A swarm scan over an empty state
reduces to the cold scan tree-for-tree. Because the seed alone no longer
determines the tree, each run archives its pheromone table — regeneration is
seed plus manifest.
